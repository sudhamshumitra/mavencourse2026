51st Annual IAEA Conference

Toronto, Ontario

September 27 to October 2, 2026

Conference Highlights

EQAO, in partnership with the International Association for Educational Assessment (IAEA) and Vretta, is proud to host IAEA’s 51 st Annual Conference in Toronto, under the theme “Trust, Transparency and Technology in Educational Assessment.”

The conference will bring together international experts, researchers, practitioners and policy-makers to explore how assessment systems can be designed and delivered to be more equitable, innovative and trustworthy in a rapidly changing world.

The 2026 IAEA conference will address a wide range of topics related to assessment, including culturally responsive and fair practices; transparency in scoring and measurement; student and educator voice in assessment design; data sharing; AI and machine learning; privacy and security, and the balance between accountability and support in education systems.

This event will provide an excellent platform for sharing experiences, advancing research, strengthening professional collaboration and building global partnerships. EQAO is committed to ensuring that the conference is a productive, engaging and memorable experience for all participants.

Keynote Speakers

Meet the voices driving change. This year’s keynote speakers bring expertise, real‑world experience and fresh perspectives to inspire new thinking and spark meaningful conversations.

Dr. Eunice Eunhee Jang

Professor at the Ontario Institute for Studies in Education, University of Toronto

Dr. Jang’s work is grounded in the belief that assessment shapes who students become, not just what they learn. Her research is conducted in close partnership with educators, students, families and policy makers to ensure meaningful classroom impact, and she contributed to Ontario’s Steps to English Proficiency framework, influencing how K–12 schools assess and support multilingual learners. Dr. Jang has become a leading voice on Artificial Intelligence (AI) in education, advancing transparent and equity-centred assessment design, and she has developed AI-supported systems that provide actionable feedback while strengthening learner agency, metacognition and human-centred decision making.

Dr. Kadriye Ercikan

Senior Vice-President of Global Research at Educational Testing Service (ETS) and Professor Emerita at the University of British Columbia

Dr. Ercikan leads the foundational and innovation research programs at ETS. Contracting for national and international agencies such as the National Assessment for Educational Progress in the United States and Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development assessments such as Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA), she also leads assessment capability building and modernization efforts in countries like India and Kazakhstan. Dr. Ercikan’s research focuses on validity and fairness issues in assessment, including in innovative digital assessments using response process data, AI applications, personalization and adaptivity.

Dr. Mary Richardson

Professor of Educational Assessment at the University College London Institute of Education (IOE)

Dr. Richardson’s extensive experience includes education practice and policy, education history and testing, and assessment practice. She is presently a co-principal investigator reporting on PISA 2025 for Wales, Northern Ireland and England, and her current research is anchored in assessment with a focus on student experiences, how assessment shapes learner identity, the ethics of assessment and the future impact of generative AI. Additionally, Dr. Richardson sits on advisory boards and committees such as Qualifications Wales, and she is the co-convenor of the British Educational Research Association’s Special Interest Group for Curriculum and Assessment. She has worked on a range of research projects in Europe, Asia and America, and is the co-founder of IOE’s Educational Assessment research group.

Dr. Thierry Rocher

Director of Evaluation at the Department of Evaluation, Forecasting and Performance (DEPP)

Dr. Thierry Rocher is the director of evaluation at DEPP, the statistical office of France’s Ministry of Education. Dr. Rocher works as a statistician and holds a Ph.D. in psychology, specializing in educational measurement and psychometrics. Dr. Rocher has also contributed to the development of numerous standardized assessment programs for student in France and has held various leadership roles in the field of international educational comparisons, notably serving as the president of the International Association for the Evaluation of Educational Achievement from 2018 to 2024.

Call for Abstracts

The 51 st IAEA Annual Conference, hosted in Toronto, Canada, from Sunday, September 27, to Friday, October 2, 2026, will bring together global leaders, researchers, practitioners and policy-makers to explore the theme Trust, Transparency and Technology in Educational Assessment.

We invite submissions of abstracts that explore the conference theme through the following subthemes:

Trust

Culturally Responsive Assessment Practices:

Designing assessments that reflect diverse cultural contexts and promote inclusion.

Student and Educator Voice in Assessment Design:

Prioritizing collaborative approaches that engage learners and educators in the assessment process.

Accountability vs. Support: Balancing Stakeholder Expectations:

Exploring how assessment systems can ensure quality while also supporting growth and development.

Transparency

Data Sharing and Reporting in Educational Assessments:

Promoting clarity, accessibility and responsible communication of assessment results.

Transparency in Assessment Design and Scoring:

Making assessment methods, criteria and scoring processes open and understandable.

Equity and Fairness in Assessment:

Ensuring impartiality and fairness across diverse student populations and contexts.

Technology

AI and Machine Learning in Assessment Design and Scoring:

Harnessing intelligent systems to enhance efficiency, accuracy and personalization.

Digital Assessments: Opportunities and Challenges:

Examining innovation, accessibility and practical implementation in digital environments.

Privacy and Security in Technology-Enabled Assessment:

Safeguarding data and maintaining ethical standards in technology-driven assessment systems.

This conference will serve as a powerful platform for dialogue, research sharing and collaboration as we collectively shape the future of assessment worldwide. We welcome submissions from researchers, practitioners, policy-makers and industry experts that align with the conference theme and subthemes.

Join us in Canada as we explore how, through transparency and technology, to build assessment systems that learners, educators and societies can trust.

Presentation Sessions and Panel Discussions

There are three formats for these sessions, as shown above.

Abstracts for presentation sessions and panel discussions should state the purpose, methodology, key findings or insights and implications for educational assessment in relation to the conference theme. Notification of acceptance will be provided to the presenters.

All conference abstract submissions should include the following:

• Presentation format: presentation session (30 minutes), presentation session (45 minutes) or panel discussion (1 hour 30 minutes), as shown in the table above

• Name, organization and e-mail for each presenter

• Title of the presentation

• Conference subtheme (select one of Trust, Transparency or Technology)

• Maximum 350-word abstract

Accepted authors will be required to submit full papers and presentations in the following format:

• Font: Times New Roman, 12 pt.

• Line spacing: 1.5

• Referencing style: APA 7th edition

• Name of the main presenter

• Selected subtheme

Full papers should reflect original work and align with the conference theme: Trust, Transparency and Technology in Educational Assessment.

Poster Presentations

Poster presentations will take place on the last day of the conference. Each presenter will be allotted 10 minutes for an oral summary of their poster. Posters must present original research or projects conducted by the presenters and may include ongoing work. All poster content must be in English.

Poster proposal submissions should include:

• 200-word abstract

• title and presenters

• introduction

• methodology

• results

• discussion and conclusions

The conference organizers will provide a standardized poster template to maintain a uniform display.

• Maximum poster text: 500 words

• Images/figures: Up to three

• Presenter(s) photo (a colour portrait photo of each presenter must be included)

Niagara Region Day Trip

Join fellow conference attendees for a guided full-day excursion to two of Ontario’s most iconic destinations: Niagara Falls and Niagara-on-the-Lake. This optional experience offers a perfect opportunity to connect, explore and take in the natural beauty, history and culture of the Niagara region.

Participants and their guests will have the opportunity to experience Niagara Falls, often regarded as one of the world’s greatest natural wonders, and explore the surrounding attractions. The day also includes time in the charming heritage town of Niagara-on-the-Lake, known for its picturesque streets, boutique shops, wineries and warm Canadian hospitality.

Excursion Highlights

• Engaging guided excursion with a licensed tour guide 

• Stunning scenic views of Niagara Falls 

• Visits to popular Niagara Parks attractions, including a Niagara Falls boat cruise 

• Enjoy a provided lunch during the excursion 

• Discover and explore the Niagara-on-the-Lake region 

• Meaningful opportunities to connect with colleagues in a relaxed setting

Conference Venue

Located in the vibrant heart of Toronto, the Marriott Downtown at CF Toronto Eaton Centre offers the perfect blend of convenience, comfort and sophistication, making it the ideal venue for the 51 st Annual IAEA Conference.

Just steps from premier shopping, dining and cultural attractions, the hotel provides access to the best of Toronto. Delegates can easily explore iconic landmarks such as Nathan Phillips Square, the CN Tower, Rogers Centre, St. Lawrence Market, Scotiabank Arena, the Hockey Hall of Fame, Ripley’s Aquarium and Union Station, which are all within walking distance. For international and domestic travellers, the hotel’s central location ensures stress-free transportation options. Union Station is just minutes away and offers the UP Express train to Toronto Pearson International Airport in approximately 25 minutes. Billy Bishop Toronto City Airport is also conveniently located only two miles away.

Conference Venue Accommodations

A block of rooms has been reserved exclusively for conference delegates at a deeply discounted rate, significantly lower than regular prices during Toronto’s busy autumn season.

Rooms are limited and in high demand. We strongly encourage delegates to register early and secure their accommodations as soon as possible.

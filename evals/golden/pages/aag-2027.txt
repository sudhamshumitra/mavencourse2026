Deadlines and Submissions

Please note: The AAG has changed the submission process this year:

• Paper presenters and panelists will submit through the same abstracts/panelists portal. You do not need to be assigned to a session to submit an abstract, but all panelists must have previously agreed to serve on a specific panel.

• Abstract submissions that are not assigned to a session by December 17 will convert to poster presentations.

• Session submissions will remain the same as in previous years.

The AAG accepts all submitted abstracts and organized sessions for presentation. The registration fee must be paid prior to abstract submission. You may only submit one abstract for presentation and serve as a panelist in one panel session. If you opt not to submit an abstract, you may be a panelist twice.

• October 15: Abstract submission deadline

• November 5: Session organizing deadline

Themes

Grounded Relations and Desirable Futures

Our meeting in New York City takes place in uncertain times that challenge the values that make us human—care for life, love of beauty, and appreciation for the earth. What better setting to confront this challenge than a complex, diverse city of nearly 9 million people, with an energy that juxtaposes past and future on every street corner? This year’s theme, Grounded Relations and Desirable Futures, grapples with the history, relationships, and potential of societies and the Earth, asking what part geographers play in healing or harming them.

Geographers are well positioned to think across disciplinary boundaries, to create and use tools critically and in partnership with local communities, and to consider the most important questions of our day. We both inherit and transform the methods, techniques, and tools once made for exploration and empire, which can also be turned toward repair, reciprocity, and restoration.

This year as we gather in Lenapehoking, homeland of the Lenape People, and New York, a place of rich history and cultural tradition for many peoples, we seek to deepen our ethical relations to one another and the institutions we are part of, and work collectively to build a disciplinary home for community and the protection of our planet, making a world in which all people and life can thrive with dignity and self-determination.

Geographic Methods, Tools, and Techniques for Knowledge and Justice

Geographers access wide-ranging and extensive space- and place-based methods. What must we watch for to prevent their use for violence and surveillance? How can we turn them toward shared liberation?

Science for People and the Planet

Planetary-scale ecological crisis is made worse by politically motivated challenges such as funding cuts and restrictions on data access. Rapidly developing technologies such as AI also raise new ethical and environmental equity questions. How can we put our collective expertise to work to navigate these challenges and protect our planet?

Geographies of Repair and Liberation

In conversation with the work of the 2026-2028 Presidential Taskforce, this theme invites us to reckon with and transform geography’s institutional and disciplinary relations to Indigenous dispossession, the afterlives of slavery, and accountability for modern-day effects of systemic, historic forces. Whose land are we on? Who built and maintains our buildings? Whose knowledge do we rely on, and how do we share credit and benefit? Presenters are asked to consider our ethical relations to the places and people who make life possible.

Desirable Futures

The future is often upheld as a goal and a vision, but who is included and who is not? What do we learn when we make time part of our means of analysis? This theme invites us to reflect and build on the temporal turn in geography, but it is also a call to reshape the intimate geopolitics of our relations through how we take up and allocate time in community with one another.

World Geography Bowl

Don’t miss this exciting annual quiz bowl tournament featuring teams of graduate and undergraduate geography students representing each of the AAG’s regional divisions. The tournament is fully staffed by volunteers, who write and edit questions and help run the proceedings as judges, scorekeepers and moderators.

Field Visits and Workshops

Go Beyond the Session Schedule

Help shape the annual meeting experience by organizing a field visit or workshop. These opportunities allow you to extend conversations beyond traditional sessions—whether by guiding peers through the host city or leading an interactive learning experience. Organized by communities, partners, and sponsors, field visits and workshops create meaningful spaces for connection, collaboration, and hands-on engagement.

Field Visits

Design and lead an experience that brings geography to life in the host city. Whether highlighting local landscapes, cultural geographies, or community-based geographic work, these small-group excursions allow you to create memorable, place-based learning experiences.

Field visits require pre-registration due to limited space availability. Attendee fees cover activity costs and administrative support.

TIMELINE

Thursday, November 5 — Form submission deadline: Any requests submitted after the deadline will be approved on a case-by-case basis.

Thursday, November 5 – Friday, November 20 — Confirm logistics: Laura Palmer will be in touch with questions

Friday, December 4 — Website listing content approval: Organizers will be emailed the details that will be posted online and asked to make edits or approve

Thursday, December 10 — Field visit listings published on website

FEES

Any cost associated with running a field visit (museum tickets, tour guide honorarium, snacks, etc.) are totaled and passed along to the attendees. In addition to the hard costs, each field visit will be charged a 25% administrative fee on the total cost of the field visit.

Honorarium will be paid based on active signups. Any honorarium rate will be agreed to prior to the opening of the field visit registration.

Please keep in mind that cost-effective experiences tend to be the most popular, and low attendance may lead to cancellation.

WHAT’S INCLUDED

Field visits will be mentioned in several AAG promotional emails

WHAT’S NOT INCLUDED

• NYC has great public transportation that is user-friendly and environmentally friendly. AAG will rely on public transit rather than buses for events this year.

• NYC subway fare is paid using OMNY, a contactless payment system. Simply tap your contactless credit card, debit card, or smartphone digital wallet (like Apple Pay or Google Pay) directly on the turnstile’s OMNY reader to enter. Standard rides cost $3.

• If you plan to use the subway or other public transit, please communicate this in the field visit description.

• Any questions should be directed to lpalmer@evergreenmeetings.com.

• We do not recommend adding meal costs into the total cost of the field visit as it complicates the restaurant selection and ordering process

• If your field visit runs over a mealtime, you will need to address that the meal is an additional cost in the field visit description

• It is typical practice for field visit organizers to pay the logistical expenses (honorariums, snacks, etc.) and be reimbursed after the conference.

AAG may not market individual field visits to fill space—you are welcome to promote your workshop as you see fit.

Submit your field visit

Workshops

Workshops provide a platform to share knowledge through interactive, hands-on learning. These sessions are ideal for teaching practical skills, demonstrating tools, or guiding attendees through specialized topics in a collaborative setting.

Workshops can be longer or shorter than general sessions and require pre-registration due to limited space availability. Attendee fees cover workshop costs and administrative support.

TIMELINE

Thursday, November 5 — Form submission deadline: Any requests submitted after the deadline will be approved on a case-by-case basis.

Thursday, November 5 – Friday, November 20 — Confirm logistics: Laura Palmer will be in touch with questions

Friday, December 4 — Website listing content approval: Organizers will be emailed the details that will be posted online and asked to make edits or approve

Thursday, December 10 — Workshop listings published on website

FEES

Each workshop will be charged a $750 facility fee. This cost can either be sponsored by your organization, an AAG Specialty Group, or passed along and divided among the attendees as the registration fee.

To have the fees covered by an AAG Specialty Group, you must provide an email from the Specialty Group chair giving their written consent to use Specialty Group funds. The email should be sent to lpalmer@evergreenmeetings.com.

WHAT’S INCLUDED

• Room set in classroom seating (chairs at a rectangular table)

• Screen, projector, microphone, sound

• Workshops will be mentioned in several AAG promotional emails

WHAT’S NOT INCLUDED

• Internet is not complimentary in workshop rooms and is usually prohibitively expensive. If internet is necessary, it is recommended that you order one connection for an instructor demonstration rather than ordering internet for each attendee. If internet for each attendee is absolutely necessary, please indicate the number of connections in the Per-Person Costs section and AAG will obtain a quote for you.

• AAG does not provide handouts or any additional materials for the workshops

• Any food and beverage or additional AV equipment will be added to the total cost of hosting the workshop

AAG may not market individual workshops to fill space—you are welcome to promote your workshop as you see fit.

Submit your workshop

NODES

Host an AAG Node

AAG will work with key institutions with the goal of hosting nodes for the 2027 meeting, many with their own unique programming, as well as access points and coordinated activities for the annual meeting. Each node will be based on the “watch party” concept, with an optional component for participating directly in the conference through virtual sessions. To host a node or learn more, contact meeting@aag.org .

Accessibility and Inclusion

The AAG works to create an Annual Meeting that is accessible to all. This includes reasonable accommodations as specified by the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA). AAG and its conference venues ensure mobility access within all session and conference rooms. An accessibility request question is available during the registration process and can also be sent to meeting@aag.org .

Justify Your Trip

Requesting funding from your university, company, or organization to attend the Annual Meeting? The AAG provides language below for all prospective attendees to use to help with the funding request process.

Reasons to attend

• Become part of an international community of geographers. Join thousands of

geographers and related professionals from 60+ countries who attend the Annual Meeting.

• Present your research. Showcase your work at any stage for constructive feedback and advice with a paper or poster presentation.

• Collaborate and connect. Engage with other scholars in the field, explore potential

collaborations, and make lifelong connections with important figures in the discipline.

• Broaden your horizons. Discover the many facets of geography by meeting new people and attending sessions on unfamiliar topics.

• Never stop learning. Earn continuing education credit for your professional licenses or accreditation by participating in workshops, presenting your research, and attending sessions.

• Network to get work. Attend career mentoring sessions, visit our Jobs & Careers Center, and participate in professional development workshops. The Exhibit Hall also features dozens of organizations from academia, government, and the private sector that are looking to hire talented geographers.

• Stay up-to-date. Keep an eye on current technologies and best practices in your facet of the discipline at technical workshops, the Exhibit Hall, and cutting-edge session presentations.

• Explore! The AAG Annual Meeting changes locations each year, allowing attendees to explore and discover new places in addition to all the conference has to offer.

Travel to the United States

Invitation Letter

The AAG provides a formal invitation letter to help with the visa submission process. The general visa form letter will allow you to fill out your contact information, then save or print a copy for your records.

If you have already registered and would like your abstract and/or session title included in your invitation letter, please use the custom visa form.

Download a general visa letter                 Download a custom visa letter

Visa Information

Visa requirements often change and vary by country, so be sure to visit the U.S. State Department website for the latest information when planning your trip. If you need a visa, be sure to apply early. We recommend applying three to four months before your date of travel to allow sufficient time to process your application.

Travelers entering the United States under the Visa Waiver Program need a valid ESTA approval. The Electronic System for Travel Authorization (ESTA) is an online application process used to determine the eligibility of visitors to travel to the United States under the Visa Waiver Program and ESTA .

Additional tips and information about visa requirements are available from The National Academies International Visitors Office .

Resources for Travelers

Electronic Frontier Foundation information on digital privacy at the U.S. border, with a long version and short version of guidance for travelers.

Information on international travelers’ rights and responsibilities in the U.S. from CLEAR , the free legal assistance program at the City University of New York.

For LGBT2QIA+ travelers, in particular gender-nonconforming and trans travelers, resources on flying to and in the U.S. (scroll down) from Advocates 4 Trans Equality.

COVID-19 Information

AAG’s first priority is the health and safety of Annual Meeting attendees and partners. Our goal is to hold a safe event, recognizing it is not possible to remove all risks, in particular concerning COVID-19.

In keeping with local and state guidelines and policies, AAG will not require attendees to show proof of vaccination or wear masks during Annual Meeting events. We recommend that attendees receive all available vaccinations and boosters in advance of the meeting, and wear masks when participating in indoor activities. Masks and hand sanitizer will be available to attendees throughout the meeting spaces.

AAG will follow CDC public health recommendations, and federal, state, and local regulations applicable at the time of the AAG Annual Meeting. AAG will put policies and measures in place to provide a safe environment for all attendees, and AAG may change, update, or add to these requirements at any time as it deems prudent to best protect the health and safety of attendees and others.

Submit an
abstract
(now closed)
7-9 December 2026, Cairo, Eastern Mediterranean Region

Abstract submissions are now closed.
Across all four sub-themes , there are three session types in which you can submit an abstract:

• Individual sessions

• Organized sessions

• Capacity strengthening sessions.
The Symposium also seeks to build the field of health systems research, in addition to addressing important thematic areas.
Sessions that allow for an examination of a common set of problems, develop or advance a body of theory and knowledge, and/or consider common practices, are therefore welcome.
In order to foster diversity, each person will be permitted to present a maximum of:

• Once in an organized or capacity strengthening session (either as chair/moderator or named contributor) and

• Once in individual, abstract-based sessions.
The Scientific Committee will review multiple submissions that include the same-named contributor, but the Program Working Group will be responsible for finalizing abstract selection to maximize diversity and ensure balance across the program.
Languages:  Abstracts in English, the working language of the conference, will be accepted.
Submission Deadlines (now closed):

• Organized – application deadline 12:00pm (GMT) 13 February 2026

• Capacity Strengthening – application deadline 12:00pm (GMT) 13 February 2026

• Individual – application deadline: 12am(GMT+1) on Sunday 19 April 2026
Application outcomes (have now been shared)
Applicants will be informed of the outcome of their application as follows:

• Organized Sessions: Early April 2026

• Capacity Strengthening Sessions: Early April 2026

• Individual Sessions: Late July 2026
Note: Applicants accepted for an Individual oral or poster presentation must confirm their intention to attend and present at HSR2026 by 27 August 2026 at 23:59 GMT+1 .

Submit an abstract

Individual session: Closed
Individuals can submit abstracts as a paper presentation.
A paper is a 10 minute oral presentation including time allowed for questions.
If your submission does not qualify for the oral presentation, we may consider you for the flash oral program. This requires the creation of a physical poster that will be displayed across the 3 days. On one of the symposium days, you will have an opportunity to present your poster via a 3-minute lightning talk. 
Those submitting individual abstracts will be required to submit the following information:

• Full title (max 150 characters including spaces)

• Short title for communications (max 75 characters including spaces)

• Speaker contact details

• HSR2026 sub-theme

• A list of co-authors for outlining in the abstract book, if applicable

• An abstract of 3,000 characters including spaces or less

• A shorter abstract summary for the conference program and app of 750 characters including spaces or less.

Abstract format
A structured abstract should be prepared for empirical research presentations, covering background, methods, results and discussion/conclusions.
For conceptual research or work under other field-building dimensions, abstracts should cover Purpose, focus/content, significance for the sub-theme area/field-building dimension of relevance and target audience.
Individual abstracts may be accepted either for oral, multimedia or poster presentation.
*Note – oral submissions will automatically be considered for posters so there is no need to submit twice.
Evaluation criteria
In line with the core principles of the Symposium, the Scientific Committee will be asked to assess individual abstracts based on:

• Technical merit

• Relevance to the Symposium theme

• Significance to the sub-theme area and field-building dimension.

Organized session: closed
Organizers may submit proposals for 60 or 90-minute sessions under two different ‘session type’ categories:

• Participatory sessions: These could be round table discussions, debates, ‘fishbowl’ discussions, simulations, games, pyramid sessions, group modelling, or any other approach that actively encourages audience participation

• Panel presentations: This format is the traditional panel presentation, but organizers must allow adequate time for audience discussion and interaction.
The organized session template requires the following information:

• Title (maximum 25 words)

• Session type

• Thematic area and field-building dimension

• The session organizer’s contact details

• Contributors’ details: a maximum of 8 session contributors

• Speaker details: a maximum of 8 people

• A short (50-word) overview of the organized session that will appear in the Symposium program

• A short summary of your abstract for communications purposes in the conference program and the Symposium app (max 750 characters including spaces). This will help delegates to select relevant sessions of their interest.

• A summary of session content: purpose/objective, technical focus, target audience, and relevance to the selected theme (max 3,000 characters including spaces).

Evaluation criteria
In line with the core Symposium principles, the Scientific Committee will be asked to assess organized sessions on the basis of:

• Technical merit

• Relevance to the Symposium theme

• Significance for the sub-theme area and/or field-building dimension

• Engagement of policy-makers, managers and civil society groups (i.e. chair and those with planned roles)

• Potential for active involvement by the audience.

Capacity strengthening session closed
Capacity strengthening is a core element of our mission, and related sessions have become a regular and very popular feature of the HSR symposia. Taking place during the main conference days, these sessions address diverse topics and provide important opportunities to share experiences.
Our overall goal is to contribute to developing the field of Health Policy and Systems Research (HPSR). We also seek to serve the needs of our diverse membership, including decision-makers, practitioners, advocates, educators and researchers.
We start with the understanding that there are three interlinked layers of capacity for HPSR: individual, organizational and network.
While individual-level skill-building is important, HPSR field-building will require capacity strengthening activities that also address the organizational and/or network levels.
We encourage sessions that:

• Consider the organizational and network levels of capacity strengthening

• Address audiences other than researchers/evaluators

• Tackle topics other than those focused on research issues or methods (for example, teaching and learning, advocacy, leadership for learning health systems, and more)

Although we encourage these abstracts to consider the overall Symposium theme and sub-themes, this is not required for capacity strengthening abstracts.
Organizers may submit proposals for 60 or 90-minute sessions under two different ‘session type’ categories:

• Participatory sessions: These could be round table discussions, debates, ‘fishbowl’ discussions, simulations, games, pyramid sessions, group modeling, or any other approach that actively encourages audience participation

• Panel presentations: This format is the traditional panel presentation, but organizers must allow adequate time for audience discussion and interaction.
The capacity strengthening session template requires the following information:

• Full title (maximum 150 characters including spaces)

• Short title for communications (maximum 75 characters including spaces)

• Primary contact information

• Contributors’ details: a maximum of 8 session contributors

• Speaker details: a maximum of 8 people

• Session type

• HSR2026 sub-theme area

• Session intended audience

• Capacity level of focus

• Identifying which aspects of the HSG capacity strengthening conceptual framework your abstract best addresses

• A short overview of the capacity strengthening session for the conference program and app of 750 characters including spaces or less

• Why is strengthening capacity in this area important? And how is it relevant and linked to aspects of the HSG Conceptual Framework? (max 2,250 characters including spaces)

• What THREE key learning outcomes will the session have? (max 750 characters including spaces)

• How will the session support capacity strengthening? (max 2,250 characters including spaces)

• What output(s) will the session generate to share with participants and with the wider HSG community afterwards? (max 750 characters including spaces)
Evaluation criteria
In line with the core Symposium principles, the Scientific Committee will be asked to assess capacity strengthening sessions on the basis of:

• Technical merit

• Relevance to the Symposium theme

• Significance for the sub-theme area

• Clarity of learning outcomes and appropriateness for primary target audiences

• Clarity of relevance and link to aspects of the HSG Conceptual Framework for systemic capacity strengthening for health policy
and systems research

• Format and participant engagement – feasibility and likely engagement levels

• Clear, useful and feasible outputs

• Appropriateness of and diversity among session leaders, especially representing LMICs

• Focuses on the organizational and/or network levels of capacity strengthening, or more than one level
